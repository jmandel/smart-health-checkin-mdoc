Kiosk handoff demo: same-device protocol, deployment-defined UX

The front-desk kiosk demo is not a second SMART Health Check-in 1.0 protocol layer. It shows one deployment-defined way for staff to start an in-person visit on a desktop while the patient lands on a same-device verifier page and presents from their phone.

Mental model

The same-device verifier already demonstrates the core presentation: a requester builds a SMART Health Check-in request, invokes navigator.credentials.get(), the patient approves in their wallet, and the requester opens and validates the response.

Spec boundary: SMART Health Check-in 1.0 standardizes the transport-neutral SMART request/response model and the same-device direct org-iso-mdoc presentation flow. QR URLs, pointers, relays, desktop completion screens, and response-return paths shown here are demo/deployment behavior, not standardized pointer, envelope, relay, submission, or completion protocols.

The kiosk demo keeps that exact phone-side presentation. The desktop creates a check-in session; the phone opens a session-bound context for that visit, lets the patient share from their wallet, and returns the result to the waiting kiosk session. The patient still uses their own phone and health app to present.

In-person handoff demo

The desktop starts a session and displays a QR. The phone opens the session context, runs the same presentation, and returns the SMART response for the desktop to render.

What happens in the kiosk demo

  1. Kiosk starts a check-in session. Staff start the visit on a shared desktop. The kiosk prepares the SMART request for that session and displays a QR or link that the patient can open on their own phone.
  2. The phone opens a session-bound context. The QR or link gets the patient to a phone page associated with the kiosk session. The URL shape, session establishment mechanism, and return channel are deployment-defined; they are not SMART Health Check-in 1.0 protocol elements.
  3. The phone recovers the request for review. Once the session context is established, the phone page obtains the SMART request for that visit and presents the requested sharing in a private, patient-controlled context.
  4. The phone runs the same presentation flow. The phone invokes the normal same-device Digital Credentials API flow, the patient approves in their wallet, and the phone validates the returned SMART Health Check-in response.
  5. The kiosk receives the session result. The phone returns the SMART response to the waiting kiosk session through deployment-defined infrastructure. The kiosk checks the response against the original request before rendering or using it.

The handoff contract

The public contract is intentionally small: a kiosk session can invite a phone into a deployment-authenticated or session-bound context; the phone can perform the normative same-device sharing flow; and the accepted SMART response can be returned to the waiting kiosk session.

1. Start Kiosk creates a visit-scoped sharing session.
2. Join Phone opens a context bound to that session.
3. Share Phone runs the same-device wallet presentation.
4. Return Kiosk receives and validates the session result.
What the handoff proves: a phone page and kiosk page are participating in the same deployment session, and the returned SMART response matched the original request.
What it does not prove by itself: requester identity, organization authority, clinical-source provenance, patient matching, downstream EHR write-back authorization, or payment/claims acceptance.

What deployments choose

Different deployments can choose different ways to establish the phone context and return the result: QR codes, app links, staff-assisted lookup, authenticated web sessions, polling, push, or server-mediated relay. Those choices are deployment plumbing, not new SMART Health Check-in wire protocols.

Keep the boundary clear: companion demos may document a concrete relay for implementation purposes, but the public protocol story should stay at the session-handoff level unless a deployment profile chooses to standardize more.

The invariant across those choices is that the phone performs the normative same-device presentation, and the kiosk treats the returned payload as a session-bound SMART response to validate against the original request.
